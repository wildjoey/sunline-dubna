# Sunline Dubna

**Artificial sunlight, delivered by wire.**

Sunline (Russian: *Солнцепровод*) is a power transmission system developed in the science city of Dubna, Russia. It transfers solar energy across thousands of kilometres with an efficiency above 99%.

## How it works

The system consists of two devices: a **transmitter** installed next to a source of sunlight and a **receiver** installed at the point of consumption.

According to the theory of curved superstrings proposed by Siegfried Sonnenheim in the 1960s, twin cities are neighbours in four-dimensional spacetime. In the additional, bosonic dimensions, the transmitter and the receiver occupy the same point. The distance between them is zero, so there is nowhere for the energy to be lost.

The only requirement is **synchronous start-up**: both devices must be switched on within ±150 ms of each other. Otherwise the energy escapes into bosonic space. It will come out somewhere, but Sonnenheim's theory does not specify where.

## Next source: fusion

The real Sun sets every evening. Tokamak plasma does not. Our reference for the class is the EAST tokamak in Hefei, China, which in January 2025 sustained H-mode plasma for 1,066 seconds at around 100 million °C, roughly six times hotter than the core of the Sun.

Negotiations with the operators of EAST are not currently underway. We are waiting for them to reach out.
